ML/AI
Dhruv Srikanth, Bingchen Zhao, Dixing Xu, Yuxiang Wu, Zhengyao Jiang (Weco AI) · arXiv:2609.26457 · Sep 22, 2026

Not just a proposal but a working demonstration: an agent that edits its own code, benchmarks the modified version of itself against hidden AI-R&D evaluation tasks, and keeps only the changes that win. Left running autonomously for 8 days with no human in the loop, it found seven successive self-improvements — new search policies, new memory mechanisms — each one measurably better than the last. Greedy Decoding Is Not Precision-Invariant: Cross-Precision Output Divergence in LLM Inference

ML/AI
Gaoyuan Du, Anam Nawaz Khan, Rex Zhou, et al. · arXiv:2609.26621 · Sep 22, 2026 (accepted to TMLR)

Greedy decoding is assumed to be deterministic, but this paper shows the same model, prompt, and hardware produce different outputs under BF16 versus FP16 — with 49–100% of prompts diverging across six models from 1.1B to 12B parameters. A single flipped token early on can cascade into a completely different reasoning trajectory. A quietly important finding: "reproducible" LLM outputs and benchmark scores turn out to depend on numerical precision choices that papers rarely report.

Harm Laundering in GPT Models

ML/AI
Sarah Wyer, Sue Black, Noura Al Moubayed (Durham University) · arXiv:2609.20779 · Sep 17, 2026

Analyzing 450,000 completions across 15 GPT model generations, the authors argue that as safety training reduces explicit harmful outputs, gender-discriminatory content doesn't disappear — it mutates into subtler forms that surface-level toxicity classifiers miss entirely. They formalize this "harm laundering" as a testable, three-criteria phenomenon, directly challenging the industry habit of citing declining benchmark harm scores as evidence of real progress.

ML/AI
Chetan Pathade, Prathamesh Pawar, Shubham Patil · arXiv:2609.25173 · Sep 21, 2026

A meta-analysis of 259 agentic-security papers published between Feb 2025 and Sep 2026 finds that "Attack Success Rate," the field's default headline metric, is really six unspecified design choices dressed up as one number — roughly 60% of papers report no variance or repeated runs, and fewer than a third even disclose whether their evaluation setup was stochastic. A methodological gut-check likely to reshape how agent-jailbreak papers get reviewed and compared.

Math

Resolves four instances of the classical "no-three-in-line" problem — two cube variants and two planar Guy-Kelly variants — via exact rational decision procedures run inside symmetry strata, with every computer-generated witness independently re-verified by a second, code-independent checker. Poisson Exchange Beyond Submodularity: Effective Approximation Algorithms for Subset Selection over Matroids

Math
Shi Fu, Youming Qiao, Dacheng Tao, Zongqi Wan, Qixin Zhang · arXiv:2609.24569 · Sep 21, 2026

Introduces "MGPE," an algorithm built on a non-homogeneous Poisson exchange clock, that gets strong approximation guarantees for matroid-constrained subset selection under γ-weak submodularity — a much more general condition than the classical submodularity assumption — in both offline and online/bandit settings. Weak-submodular objectives show up throughout feature selection, neural-net pruning, and video summarization, exactly where the classical theory doesn't apply.

A Stein Proof of the Burke Property for the Stationary O'Connell–Yor Polymer

Math
Ruixuan Zhang · arXiv:2609.18214 · ~Sep 15–16, 2026

Gives a new, more conceptual proof of the Burke property — a stationarity/independence structure central to the O'Connell-Yor directed random polymer — using Stein's method, and derives a multilevel joint Gaussian-Gamma Stein identity along the way. Stein's-method proofs of Burke-type properties are rare in integrable probability, and the technique looks like it could generalize to other polymer and exclusion-process models.

Claude Discovers a Novel Enzyme System with CRISPR-like Repeats

Lab
Anthropic · Sep 23, 2026

Anthropic's new life-sciences research group set roughly 950 parallel Claude agents (one planning/executing, one reviewing) loose on a database of 1.9 billion protein clusters, searching for new reverse-transcriptase systems over 21 hours and 210M tokens. The run surfaced ~200,000 candidates, narrowed to 3,500 novel systems, one of which — "ART" (array-associated reverse transcriptase) — shares structural hallmarks with only a handful of known programmable DNA-editing systems. A striking demonstration of largely autonomous agentic scientific discovery, backed by a not-yet-peer-reviewed preprint.
