Formally Verified Autoresearch for Analyzing the Expressivity of Multi-Head Attention

ML/AI
arXiv:2610.25341 · to appear at NeurIPS 2026

A frontier model was turned loose on a genuine open question in theory — how many attention heads a one-layer, attention-only transformer needs to represent a given Boolean function — and asked to generate conjectures, prove them, and have the proofs machine-checked in Lean, with humans only posing the question and reviewing results. It nails the exact head count for every symmetric Boolean function and shows a natural lower bound (threshold degree) isn't always tight. A concrete, independently-checkable data point in the "can AI produce new math" debate dominating this month's headlines — small and theorem-shaped rather than headline-shaped.

Interpreting Language Model Parameters

ML/AI
arXiv:2610.20748 · Goodfire

Most mechanistic interpretability work studies activations — what a model computes on a given input — rather than the weights themselves. This paper pushes the Goodfire team's parameter-decomposition line of work further, breaking a model's weights directly into sparse, largely independent subcomponents that each implement a recognizable piece of its learned algorithm, and scales the approach toward full language models instead of toy nets. If it holds up at scale, it's a genuinely different lens on interpretability than the activation-patching tools most of the field has converged on.

Scaling to Tens of Thousands of Test-Time Iterations with Loop-Native Attention Residuals

ML/AI
arXiv:2610.11570 · "InfiLoop"

Looped transformers — reusing the same block repeatedly at inference time instead of adding depth — are a cheap way to buy test-time compute, but accuracy normally plateaus or degrades after a few dozen iterations. This paper redesigns the residual connections to be loop-aware and reports continued accuracy gains on Sudoku-Extreme past 20,000 effective iterations, far beyond where prior looped architectures saturate — a concrete architectural fix rather than another prompting trick for the test-time-compute scaling problem.

On the Statistical Mechanics of Attention

ML/AI
arXiv:2610.17347

Continues a line of work treating softmax attention as a modern Hopfield network and borrowing tools from spin-glass physics to analyze it. This paper identifies something like a phase transition in attention's behavior — a "glassy," unstable regime versus a "robust-inference" regime, depending on how sharply attention concentrates — giving a physical-systems explanation for failure modes like attention collapse that have mostly been described empirically until now.

Training Dynamics of Transformers on Unbounded Inputs: Attention Learning Can Lag Output Layer Learning

ML/AI
arXiv:2610.20337

Studies what happens during training when input sequences can grow without bound, and finds a specific asymmetry: the output/unembedding layer learns to fit the training distribution well before the attention mechanism learns to route information correctly across long inputs. That lag helps explain why a model can look fully trained (low loss) while still generalizing poorly to inputs longer than anything seen in training — a mechanistic account of a failure mode usually just reported as "doesn't extrapolate to long context."

Near-Optimal Gap in the Lonely Runner Conjecture

Math
arXiv:2610.23790 · Number Theory / Combinatorics

The Lonely Runner Conjecture — that n runners on a circular track at distinct constant speeds will each, at some moment, be at least 1/n of the track's length away from every other runner — is one of the cleanest-sounding open problems in combinatorics, still unproven in general after decades. A 2025 breakthrough pushed the best unconditional lower bound on that minimal gap past the trivial 1/(2n) threshold for the first time in a meaningful way; this paper tightens the bound further, closing in on what's provable without resolving the full conjecture.

Small and Large Gaps Between Zeros of Dirichlet L-functions

Math
arXiv:2610.28527 · Number Theory

How tightly can consecutive zeros of a Dirichlet L-function — the zeta-function's cousins attached to arithmetic progressions — cluster together, or spread apart? Classical results of Siegel and others give bounds in both directions but leave a real gap between what's provable and what's conjectured; this paper narrows that gap for both the smallest and largest spacings, a technical advance that feeds directly into the zero-density estimates used across analytic number theory.

An Induced Subgraph Paradigm for Strongly Regular Graph Constructions

Math
arXiv:2610.22299 · Combinatorics

Strongly regular graphs — where every two adjacent vertices share the same number of common neighbors, and every two non-adjacent vertices do too — are rare, highly symmetric objects central to design theory and coding theory. Least Singular Values of Gaussian Matrices with Deterministic Shifts

Math
arXiv:2610.27008 · Probability

A random matrix shifted by a fixed deterministic matrix (rather than another random one) is harder to analyze near its spectral edge, where the smallest singular value lives — and that smallest singular value governs the matrix's invertibility and numerical condition number. This paper sharpens the known tail bounds on how small that value can get, extending a line of results tracing back to Sankar–Spielman–Teng's smoothed-analysis work that underlies why randomized numerical algorithms behave well in practice.

Cutoff Profile for the Balanced Generalized Bernoulli–Laplace Model

Math
arXiv:2610.28550 · Probability

The Bernoulli–Laplace model is a classical Markov chain describing two urns exchanging balls, famous as one of the first examples where mathematicians discovered "cutoff" — a chain that stays far from equilibrium for a long time, then collapses to near-equilibrium abruptly, in a narrow window. This paper works out the precise shape of that cutoff window, not merely that cutoff happens, for a more general balanced version of the model — the kind of sharp quantitative result that mixing-time theory for named chains doesn't always achieve.

Cuspidal Class Number Formulae for Intermediate Modular Curves of Prime Level

Math
arXiv:2610.23690 · Number Theory

Modular curves sit at the crossroads of number theory and arithmetic geometry, and their cuspidal class groups — algebraic invariants attached to the curve's "boundary" points — have had exact formulas for only a handful of the simplest cases, like X₀(p) and X₁(p). This paper extends exact class-number formulas to a broader family of intermediate modular curves at prime level, filling in curves that previously only had partial or conjectural answers.
